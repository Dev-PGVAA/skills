# Fidelity for long or mixed material

## Source ledger

For a large corpus, keep a compact working ledger: source ID/title, version/date, portion accessed, locator scheme, and gaps. Preserve document boundaries while extracting, and split long text at semantic boundaries; include adjacent context where a split could detach a qualification or pronoun from its referent.

Do not recursively compress summaries until the originals disappear. Retain a source-to-claim map through synthesis. Sampling permits a sample summary, not a claim of complete coverage. A long source's last section is not automatically more important than its beginning.

## Useful formats

| User need | Include | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| TL;DR | Main result plus the caveat needed to interpret it | Removing the condition that makes the result true |
| Executive brief | Decision, evidence, tradeoffs, uncertainties | Inventing a recommendation or projecting certainty |
| Meeting recap | Decisions; actions with owners/dates; open questions | Turning discussion into agreement |
| Research synthesis | Findings by question, evidence quality, disagreement | Treating repeated citations to one study as multiple studies |
| Study notes | Concepts, relationships, representative examples | Adding material presented as if it came from the source |
| Transcript/video | Themes or available timestamps, attributed claims | Invented timestamps, speaker identity, or visual observations |
| Chronological recap | Events and explicit changes of position | Treating relative dates as resolved without a reference date |

## Contradiction handling

Prefer a later version only when there is evidence it supersedes the earlier version. Otherwise show the conflict and its significance. Distinguish an inconsistency within a source from disagreement between speakers or sources. Maintain the strongest counterexample or dissenting evidence when its omission would change the conclusion.

## Numerical and claim audit

For each consequential assertion, check:

- Who asserts it? Is it observation, interpretation, forecast, or allegation?
- Same unit, currency, timeframe, denominator, and comparator?
- Are “up to,” “approximately,” “at least,” “not,” and conditional clauses preserved?
- Correlation still correlation? Hypothesis still hypothesis? Draft still draft?
- Does the quote actually support the claimed meaning in context?

Use arithmetic only when useful and label calculated values. Do not silently normalize incompatible measures or convert relative dates when the source date/timezone is missing.

## Compression under a hard limit

Prioritize main conclusion, decisive evidence, material exception, and actual commitments. Remove secondary examples and repeated framing first. If the limit prevents adequate coverage, provide a clearly labeled brief summary and state the most important omitted category; do not exceed the limit by attaching a second full summary. Use the requested word/character definition where specified.
