# Specificity review — remove generic choices that hurt the product

Evaluate fit, not fashionable bans. Inter, system fonts, purple, cards, gradients, conventional CTAs, and familiar dashboard patterns are not defects by themselves. Preserve deliberate brand and platform choices. Do not replace one template with another just to look different.

## What to inspect

- Does hierarchy reflect real user decisions or a symmetric template?
- Do metric cards have defined data and an action or interpretation the user needs?
- Are card boundaries, nested containers, shadows, pills, icons, and badges explaining relationships or competing with content?
- Are product screenshots and proof real? A mockup is labeled as a mockup; fictional customers, invented metrics, or blurred fake dashboards cannot act as evidence.
- Does typography fit the language, task, and existing identity? Is a display face helping expression or hurting scanning?
- Do decorative effects hide content, slow frequent actions, or create accessibility barriers?
- Does copy identify a real subject, mechanism, benefit, or action? Generic slogans such as "unlock your potential" and unsupported "enterprise-grade" claims need evidence or a rewrite.

## Refine

Inventory consequential problems, not every recognizable pattern. For each, identify the user-visible consequence, evidence, and smallest useful correction. Distinguish defect, optional taste suggestion, and an appropriate existing choice. The user does not need an exception ledger defending every normal font or border.

Make content and workflow more specific: real labels, meaningful status, source-backed proof, informative empty states, layouts shaped by actual data. A signature element can help an expressive brief, but loud decoration and logo-removal recognizability are not completion gates for operational tools.

Review the result against the original brief and actual surface using [Review](10-review.md). Avoid branding or copy changes outside the authorized scope.
