# Policy and admissions analysis

Use this domain guide only when the report concerns public rules, institutional programs, admissions, aid, or related obligations. It is a structure guide, not evidence about current institutions or policies.

## Distinctions to preserve

- Jurisdiction and authority: federal, state, institutional, and departmental rules can apply simultaneously.
- Policy status: proposed, adopted, effective, expired, or specific to an application cycle.
- Eligibility versus likelihood: meeting published requirements does not establish admission, an award, a visa, or affordability.
- Sticker price, total cost of attendance, grants, loans, work income, and net cost are different quantities. Record currency, academic year, covered period, exclusions, and obligations.
- Institutional statements versus individualized decisions: identify what an official source actually commits to and what remains discretionary.
- A shortlist entry is a research candidate unless a recommendation is supported by the user's priorities and verified constraints.

## Evidence and report shape

Start with the user's decision and relevant cycle. Build a comparable table of institutions/programs with direct official source locators and data dates. Explain decisive exceptions in prose. Verify application and payment rules, documentation, eligibility, obligations, and timing against the responsible authority before drawing conclusions.

For conflicting pages, inspect effective dates and which office owns the rule; do not silently choose the most favorable wording. Explicitly flag unresolved contradictions. If only older-cycle information exists, state that it may change and identify what needs refreshing.

A useful conclusion separates feasible next actions, items requiring later verification, and questions that only the institution can resolve. Preparing an inquiry is not permission to contact institutions, submit applications, pay fees, or claim affiliation.
