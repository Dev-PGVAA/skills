# Research Report

Produce publication-grade analytical reports of 30+ pages (~15,000-20,000+ words)
in a formal expert register.

Two style exemplars calibrated to the expected output live in `references/`:
`example-report.md` (Russian analytical report on university admission
strategy) and `example-report-usa.md` (US research and engineering portfolio
strategy). Read the closest one before writing the first report in a new
domain — they define the density, structure and tone this skill targets.
The Russian example is the canonical structural form; the USA example
deliberately adapts the skeleton to a US policy genre (relaxed title format,
prose closing instead of numbered imperatives) — when in doubt, follow the
Russian example and the canonical skeleton below.

Write the report in the user's language unless told otherwise.

---

# Non-negotiable principles

1. Research first. A long report without fresh evidence is padding with headers.
   Collect facts BEFORE outlining. If web tools are available, use them.
   If the `deep-research` skill is available, follow its evidence levels (A-E).
2. Density, not volume. Length comes from depth — mechanisms, numbers, entities,
   comparisons — never from restating the same thought in different words.
3. Precision. Every substantive claim carries a number, date, name, threshold,
   legal act, amount, or explicit hedge. Vague claims are defects.
4. Never invent facts. No fabricated laws, statistics, quotes, prices or
   companies. Uncertain → give a range and say so ("according to other data",
   "estimates vary between X and Y").
5. Professional register. Third person, analytical, no chat tone, no hype,
   no marketing language, no "I think".
6. One report = one artifact. Deliver a single well-structured `.md` file
   (convert to `.docx`/`.pdf` only if asked).

# Workflow

## Phase 0 — Scope

Fix before writing anything:

- topic and its boundaries
- audience (decision maker / expert / general professional)
- purpose (strategy, orientation, comparison, due diligence)
- target length and section count
- language

## Phase 1 — Research

Gather per subtopic:

- key entities (organizations, products, laws, programs, tools) and their specifics
- numbers: thresholds, amounts, quotas, dates, durations, percentages
- mechanisms: how the system actually works step by step
- differentiation: how key players/variants differ
- known contradictions between sources (report them, never average silently)

Build a fact sheet FIRST. The fact sheet, not the outline, is the bottleneck
of report quality.

## Phase 2 — Architecture

Plan 8-12 major sections. Allocate an explicit word budget per section
(e.g. 10 sections × 1,500-2,000 words + tables ≈ 30+ pages).
Use the canonical skeleton below, adapted to the domain — sections can be
renamed, merged or added, but the logical arc must remain.

## Phase 3 — Writing

Write sections in order, saving progressively to the file (append section by
section; do not try to hold the whole report in one response).
Prose carries the analysis; tables and lists support it.

## Phase 4 — Verification pass

Re-check every number and named entity against the fact sheet.
Mark what could not be verified. Fill thin sections with NEW facts, not words.

## Phase 5 — Self-review

Run the checklist at the end of this skill before delivering.

# Canonical report skeleton

## Title

Format: `Topic domain: analytical report on [aspect 1], [aspect 2] and [aspect 3]`
(one line, specific, no clickbait).

## Introduction — institutional / systemic framing

Open with the systemic transformation that makes the topic matter now:
global context → why the traditional/default approach no longer suffices →
the emerging mechanism answering it → which key institutions define the field →
what exactly this report covers and how it is structured.

Framing sentences of this caliber are expected:
"This analytical report presents a comprehensive study…"

## Architecture and normative status

The rules of the domain: legal acts, classifications, levels/tiers, validity
periods, obligations. Explain the fundamental dichotomies or taxonomies the
reader must grasp first.

## Catalog of key entities

Group entities into 2-4 classes (by format, function or tier).
Every entity gets a substantive paragraph: status/level, organizer, specificity,
what it grants, quirks. Not one-liners.

## Comparative tables

Any multi-entity comparison becomes a table. First column is the comparison
parameter ("Comparison parameter"). Keep table cells short; interpretation
stays in prose around the table.

## Differentiated policies of key players

One sub-block per major player (university / company / regulator / platform):
their strategy, thresholds, exceptions, incentive programs. Contrast players
against each other — this is where the report becomes decision-useful.

## Phenomenon / trend analysis

Name the central dynamic (e.g. "credential inflation"), explain the mechanism,
consequences, and the tie-breaking/ranking rules that emerge in response.

## Methodology and tooling

How practitioners actually operate in this domain: preparation tracks,
resources, professional tool stack grouped by category, each tool with
a one-line purpose. Practical, specific, names real tools.

## Financial / incentive infrastructure

Money flows: grants, scholarships, pricing, subsidies — amounts, quotas,
eligibility, AND obligations/risks attached (clawbacks, commitments).
A summary comparison table closes the section.

## Conclusion — strategic imperatives

4-6 numbered recommendations, each with a bolded imperative header
followed by a dense explanatory paragraph. No new facts — synthesis only.
Close with a one-paragraph elevation of the argument.

## Sources

When research was web-based, append a source list. Keep raw URLs out of prose.

# Length strategy (reaching 30+ pages honestly)

Legitimate expansion mechanisms, in order of preference:

1. More entities in each catalog class (each with real specifics).
2. More key players analyzed individually.
3. Deeper mechanism explanations (step-by-step, edge cases, failure modes).
4. More comparative tables across different dimensions.
5. Tool/methodology categories with individual tool paragraphs.
6. Phenomenon analysis with worked examples and numeric scenarios.
7. Obligations/risks/financial detail per program or player.

Forbidden padding (instant quality failure):

- repeating a point from an earlier section in new words
- generic background anyone in the audience already knows
- bullet lists that paraphrase what prose already said
- filler phrases ("it should be noted that", "in the modern world")
- summarizing the section at its end when nothing follows

If a section cannot be filled with real substance, cut it from the outline
and redistribute the budget — do not inflate.

# Style rules

- Formal analytical register, third person.
- Dense paragraphs: one paragraph = one complete thought + supporting specifics.
- Expand every abbreviation on first use; then use the abbreviation.
- Numbers in numerals with units and currency (83 contests, 75 points, $12,000 per year, order No. 639 dated 31 August 2025).
- Hedge patterns for uncertainty: "as a rule", "depending on the policy of a given [player]", "according to other data", "in some cases".
- Confident framing of verified structure + explicit hedging of shaky data —
  this combination is the signature of the register.
- Terminology of the domain used consistently (define once, reuse).
- No emoji, no exclamation marks, no rhetorical questions in body text.
- Prose-to-table ratio roughly 80/20: tables condense, prose explains.

---

# Fact hygiene

- Every figure traceable to the fact sheet or a source.
- Conflicting sources → show the conflict ("750 (other sources: 150–200)").
- No data available → state the gap explicitly; never paper over it.
- Time-sensitive claims anchored to a date ("in the 2025/2026 season", "as of August 2026").
- Legal/normative claims cite the act number and date when known.

---

# Self-review checklist

Before delivering, verify:

- [ ] 30+ pages / target length met with substance, not padding
- [ ] Every section has a job; none only restates others
- [ ] Each catalog entity and each key player has a substantive paragraph
- [ ] At least 3-5 comparative tables across dimensions
- [ ] Numbers, dates and thresholds present throughout — spot-checked
- [ ] Uncertainties hedged, contradictions surfaced, nothing invented
- [ ] Conclusion gives numbered strategic imperatives, no new facts
- [ ] Register formal; no chat tone, no hype, no filler phrases
- [ ] Single `.md` file, correct heading hierarchy, tables render
