# Editing patterns and genre checks

Use only the applicable checks. A construction is not an error because models sometimes use it.

| Cue | Useful edit | Preserve when |
|---|---|---|
| “pivotal”, “groundbreaking”, “seamless” | State the concrete result the source supports | The claim is substantiated or deliberately promotional |
| “experts say”, “research shows” | Name a supplied source, or flag unsupported attribution | Fidelity requires retaining a reported claim |
| “serves as”, “boasts”, “facilitates” | Prefer “is”, “has”, or a specific action | The original verb expresses a real distinction |
| “at its core”, “the real question is” | Begin with the point | It is part of the author's recognizable rhetoric |
| Decorative “highlighting/underscoring” clauses | Explain a supported consequence or cut padding | The clause carries necessary causality or qualification |
| “could potentially”, broad disclaimers | Keep the exact degree of uncertainty in fewer words | The qualification changes the claim's scope |
| Synonym cycling | Repeat the precise term | Different terms refer to different concepts |
| Uniform rhythm / forced rules of three | Let the content determine structure and length | Parallelism improves understanding or belongs to the voice |
| Passive voice | Name the actor if known and relevant | The actor is unknown, sensitive, or irrelevant |
| Excessive bold / headings / dashes | Follow the document's house style | They support navigation, emphasis, or standard grammar |
| Generic conclusion | End on the supported consequence or next action | A formal summary is required by the genre |
| Chatbot residue | Remove greetings/praise from the reusable artifact | The user is writing an actual greeting |

## Matching samples

Use samples by the intended author, not quoted third-party material in their messages. With very little evidence, describe the voice as tentative. Privately note a few observed choices and retain unusual but coherent phrasing. Do not mimic typos, fabricate personal experiences, or flatten all writers into short conversational sentences. In Russian, retain meaningful register, ё conventions, address forms, and established terminology.

## UI copy

Use short, unambiguous action labels; word counts depend on language and accessible name requirements, not a universal three-word limit. Keep the same label for the same action.

- Errors: what happened and a next step the product actually supports. Do not invent retry, undo, support contacts, or recovery paths.
- Empty states: explain the state and an available action; a completed task may need no action.
- Tooltips: explain meaning or consequences without hiding essential instructions there.
- Destructive actions: name the affected object and consequence plainly.
- Preserve placeholders, interpolation tokens, links, keyboard labels, markup, and length limits. Do not alter functional tokens to improve style.

Do not redesign flows or require skippable onboarding during a copy-only request. If clearer text depends on missing product behavior, flag that dependency separately.

## Fidelity examples

- Source: “We may ship by Friday if review passes.” Faithful: “If review passes, we may ship by Friday.” Unfaithful: “We'll ship Friday.”
- Source: “The team reported a 12% improvement in this test.” Keep the team attribution, 12%, and test scope; do not turn it into a universal product claim.
- Source: “I helped test the prototype.” Do not rewrite as “I built and launched the product.”
